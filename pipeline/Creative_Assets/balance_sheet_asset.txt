The **Balance Sheet asset** is a structured, document-style visual representation of a company’s financial position, designed as a clean, printable sheet using Manim primitives. Rather than being an animated scene, it functions as a **static financial canvas** where each row, label, and value is individually addressable. The layout mirrors a traditional balance sheet, with **Assets on the left and Liabilities & Equity on the right**, separated by a central divider. The asset is intentionally built in a modular way so that external workflows—such as RAG pipelines or animation systems—can **update values, highlight rows, or animate transitions** without altering the underlying structure.

---

### Overall Sheet Composition

The entire asset is constructed as a top-level `VGroup`, representing a single sheet of paper. This sheet acts as the **primary container**, within which all elements—header, dividers, and rows—are positioned relative to the sheet’s geometry.

The sheet has a fixed width and height, and all internal positioning is calculated using:

* The **center of the sheet** (`cx`) for column alignment
* Explicit **y-coordinate anchors** for row placement
* Left and right **column boundaries** for text alignment

This ensures that the layout behaves like a **grid-based financial document**, where spatial consistency is maintained across all rows.

---

### Paper and Border Construction

The base of the asset is a **rectangular paper background**, filled with a solid white color to simulate a clean document. This rectangle also has a visible outer border, giving it a defined boundary within the scene.

Inside this outer boundary, a slightly smaller rectangle is added as an **inner border**, creating a subtle framing effect. This inner border uses a thinner stroke and a contrasting color, providing a sense of depth and polish without cluttering the layout.

Both rectangles are grouped together and added first, establishing the **visual foundation** upon which all other elements are layered.

---

### Header Construction

At the top of the sheet sits the **header section**, composed of two text elements:

* The **company name** (title)
* The **document type** (subtitle, e.g., “Balance Sheet”)

These two elements are arranged vertically with a small spacing between them and then positioned slightly below the top edge of the paper. This creates a clear visual hierarchy:

* The title appears more prominent due to its size and color
* The subtitle reinforces the context of the document

The entire header is grouped into a single `VGroup`, allowing it to be manipulated as one unit if needed.

---

### Central Divider and Column Layout

The sheet is divided into two equal halves using a **vertical line (center divider)**. This line starts slightly below the header and extends toward the bottom of the sheet, stopping just above the lower margin.

This divider establishes the two primary sections:

* **Left side → Assets**
* **Right side → Liabilities & Equity**

Column boundaries are defined relative to the sheet’s center:

* Left column:

  * Label anchor (`al_xl`) → far left
  * Value anchor (`al_xr`) → near the center divider
* Right column:

  * Label anchor (`rl_xl`) → just right of divider
  * Value anchor (`rl_xr`) → far right

This setup ensures that:

* Labels are **left-aligned**
* Values are **right-aligned**
* Both columns mirror each other structurally

---

### Row Construction Logic

Each row in the balance sheet is constructed using a reusable helper that creates:

* A **label text object** (e.g., “CASH”)
* A **value text object** (e.g., “12,000”)

These two elements are grouped together and positioned using:

* A shared **y-coordinate** (for vertical placement)
* Separate **x-coordinates** for label and value alignment

Rows support several variations through parameters:

* **Bold styling** for headers and totals
* **Indentation** for subcategories (e.g., line items under “Current Assets”)
* **Custom font sizes** for emphasis (e.g., totals)
* **Color overrides** for headers or highlights

Each constructed row is stored in a dictionary (`row_mobjects`) using a clean key, allowing external systems to **retrieve and manipulate specific rows directly**.

---

### Left Section (Assets)

The left half of the sheet represents **Assets**, organized in a top-down hierarchy.

It begins with a section header (“ASSETS”), followed by a subheader (“CURRENT ASSETS”). Under this, individual line items such as “CASH” and “ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE” are listed with slight indentation, visually indicating that they belong to the subcategory.

Between logical groupings, **horizontal divider lines** are inserted. These lines vary in thickness to distinguish between:

* Minor separations (thin lines)
* Major totals (thicker lines)

Totals such as “TOTAL CURRENT ASSETS” and “TOTAL ASSETS” are styled in bold, with the final total using a larger font size to emphasize its importance.

Below the current assets section, a second category (“NON-CURRENT ASSETS”) is introduced, following the same structural pattern.

---

### Right Section (Liabilities & Equity)

The right half mirrors the structure of the left but represents **Liabilities and Equity**.

It begins with a combined header (“LIABILITIES & EQUITY”), followed by:

* A subcategory for **Current Liabilities**
* Individual entries such as “ACCOUNTS PAYABLE”
* A subtotal line (“TOTAL LIABILITIES”)

Below this, a second category for **Equity** is introduced, containing entries like “RETAINED EARNINGS”.

As with the left side, horizontal divider lines are used to separate sections and emphasize totals. The final row (“TOTAL LIAB. & EQUITY”) is styled prominently to match the total assets value, reinforcing the fundamental accounting identity.

---

### Divider Lines and Visual Separation

Throughout both sections, **horizontal lines** are used as separators. These are not grouped with rows but are independently added as line objects spanning the width of their respective columns.

Two levels of emphasis are created:

* **Thin lines** → minor separation between items
* **Thicker lines** → separation before totals

These lines help structure the document visually, making it easier to scan and interpret.

---

### Final Assembly

All elements—paper, header, divider, rows, and lines—are added sequentially into the main `VGroup`. The layering ensures that:

* The paper sits at the back
* Text and lines are rendered clearly on top
* The divider remains visible but unobtrusive

Because all positions are computed relative to the paper and center axis, the layout remains stable even if the sheet dimensions are adjusted.

---

### Behavioral Manipulation Capabilities

Although the asset is static, it is designed for **dynamic updates and interaction**.

Each row is stored and can be accessed using a unique key. This allows external systems to:

* **Update values** dynamically using `update_row_value()`
* Change text color (e.g., highlight gains in green or losses in red)
* Replace values without disturbing alignment

Because labels and values are separate objects, animations such as:

* Fading values in/out
* Highlighting specific rows
* Sequentially revealing financial data

can be applied at a granular level.

The entire sheet can also be animated as a single unit using the provided `animate_entry()` method, which introduces the sheet with a slight upward fade-in motion.

---

### Summary

The Balance Sheet asset is a **grid-structured financial document simulation**, built with precise positional logic and modular components. It combines a clean paper layout, hierarchical text organization, and symmetrical column design to faithfully represent a traditional balance sheet. By exposing each row and maintaining strict alignment rules, the asset enables **fine-grained control over financial data visualization**, making it highly suitable for workflows that require dynamic updates, highlighting, or staged presentation of structured information.
