SIMPLILEARN OSL 3.0 INSTRUCTIONAL STORYBOARDING GUIDELINES
Derived Strictly from guidelines.md & Simplilearn Guide (Learning Design & CE Standards)
Focused Exclusively on Instructional Storyboarding & SME Positioning
================================================================================

1. CORE INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN PHILOSOPHY (guidelines.md Section 1)
Every storyboarding decision exists to enforce LEARNING CLARITY.
- Outcome-Driven Design: Every on-screen scene and element must serve a defined learning outcome.
- Audio is Primary (Rule 1): The SME audio recording is the instructional source of truth.
  Listen to the audio first without visuals; surface and reinforce the structure already
  present in the teacher's explanation. Never invent visual structure that contradicts
  or distracts from the SME audio.
- Elimination of Split-Attention (Rule 2 & Section 3.2): Learners must NEVER split attention
  between reading on-screen paragraphs and listening to narration.
  * On-screen text (OST) must distill spoken sentences into short keywords, steps, or anchors.
  * NEVER duplicate spoken voiceover sentences verbatim.
- Relevancy Test (Rule 4): For every visual element proposed in a storyboard, validate:
  "Where does the SME talk about this?" If the SME never refers to it, remove it.

2. PEDAGOGICAL LESSON PROGRESSION (guidelines.md Section 2.1)
Every topic module (ideal duration 6–10 minutes) must follow the guided narrative arc:
context → teaching → engagement → recap

Step 1: HOOK / INTRODUCTION
- Purpose: Grabs attention, establishes real-world professional context.
- SME Framing: 100% Full-Screen SME (Full 1920x1080 canvas).
- On-Screen Text: Topic Title (5–7 words, Chicago Title Casing) and core thematic anchor.
- Pacing: Brief, energetic introduction directly addressing the learner.

Step 2: LEARNING OBJECTIVES
- Purpose: Outlines clear, measurable goals and sets expectations.
- SME Framing: 70% Left Rounded-Rectangle SME.
- Presentation Workspace: Right side of canvas.
- Lead-In Line: "By the end of this topic, you will be able to:"
- Content: 3 to 4 concise bullet cards starting with active verbs (e.g., "Build...", "Analyze...", "Configure...").

Step 3: CONCEPT EXPLANATION
- Purpose: Delivers core theoretical foundations, principles, and architectural breakdowns.
- SME Framing: 20% Top-Right Corner Picture-In-Picture (PIP) SME.
- Presentation Workspace: Full slide visual area, maintaining clearance under the PIP window.

Step 4: DEMONSTRATION OR WORKED EXAMPLE
- Purpose: Shows the concept in action through step-by-step procedures, tool walkthroughs, or code execution.
- SME Framing: 20% Top-Right Corner PIP SME.
- Pacing: Progressive reveal of workflow steps. Zoom into key details smoothly; hold steady during explanation.

Step 5: LEARNER ACTIVITY / PAUSE PROMPT (Active Learning - Section 1.2 & 2.1)
- Purpose: Prompts learners to reflect, predict an outcome, or practice before proceeding.
- SME Framing: 70% Left Rounded-Rectangle SME or Centered Prompt Container.
- Content: A focused reflection question or action challenge (e.g., "Pause & Reflect: Which architecture best fits high throughput?").

Step 6: SUMMARY & KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Purpose: Synthesizes core principles and reinforces durable understanding.
- SME Framing: 70% Left Rounded-Rectangle SME.
- Content: Right side workspace with 2–3 high-retention summary cards.

Step 7: WHAT'S NEXT (Consistent Video Closing - Section 4.4)
- Purpose: Seamless bridge to the subsequent topic in the syllabus.
- SME Framing: 70% Left Rounded-Rectangle SME.
- Mandatory Closing Syntax: Must strictly use the standard grammatical template:
  "In the next topic, you will learn about: <Action in -ing>"
  (e.g., "In the next topic, you will learn about: Evaluating model accuracy with cross-validation.")

3. SME POSITIONING & CANVAS CLEARANCE SPECIFICATIONS (guidelines.md Section 8)
All visual staging operates on a 1920x1080 (16:9 Full HD) canvas.
Because recordings are captured in natural room settings without green screen or AI matting,
storyboards must strictly choreograph content around the physical bounding boxes of the SME:

A. LAYOUT 1: SME 100% — FULL-SCREEN PRESENTER (guidelines.md Section 8.3)
- SME Coverage: Entire canvas (1920 × 1080 px).
- Storyboard Rule: No slide graphic containers or side panels. Minimal lower-third title overlay only.

B. LAYOUT 3: SME 70% — SPLIT LEFT SME + RIGHT CONTENT AREA (guidelines.md Section 8.5)
- SME Bounding Box: Left-aligned frame (width = 640 px, height = 920 px, positioned at x = 80 px, y = 80 px).
- Mandatory Corner Radius: 10 px rounded rectangle.
- Safe Content Workspace:
  * Horizontal Bounds: x = 760 px to x = 1840 px (provides minimum 40 px safety gap from SME frame).
  * Vertical Bounds: y = 80 px to y = 1000 px.
- Collision Constraint: No slide cards, text boxes, or diagrams may cross into the left zone (x < 760 px).

C. LAYOUT 2: SME 20% — TOP-RIGHT PIP + FULL CONTENT (guidelines.md Section 8.4)
- 20% SME PIP Box: Scaled to 384 × 216 px (20% of FHD) positioned in top-right corner.
- Mandatory Corner Radius: 10 px rounded rectangle.
- CRITICAL COLLISION RULES:
  * Lead-In Line Termination: The lead-in line and any top header text MUST terminate at or left of
    x = 1450 px (80% of canvas width) so it never collides with the 20% SME window.
  * Diagram & Code Workspace: Main diagrams, tables, and code walkthroughs must remain below
    y = 270 px directly underneath the PIP window.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. EDITORIAL & ON-SCREEN TEXT (OST) STANDARDS (guidelines.md Section 4)
- Slide Titles:
  * Casing: Chicago Manual of Style (Title Casing).
  * Word Count: 5 to 7 words maximum. Avoid one-word titles.
  * Question Titles: Allowed sparingly, but avoid starting with "Why"
    (e.g., "Why Is Sales Forecasting Necessary?" -> "Need for Sales Forecasting").
  * Demo/Activity Titles: Start with an "-ing" verb (e.g., "Analyzing Performance Data").
- Lead-In Lines & Bullet Points:
  * Language: US English, active voice, simple present tense.
  * Tone: Objective and instructional. Avoid conversational phrases in OST (e.g., avoid "Now let's see an example").
  * Prohibited Forms: Avoid abbreviations and contractions (e.g., avoid "e.g.", "i.e.", "&", "etc.", "don't").
  * Numbers Rule: Spell out single-digit numbers 0 through 9 in words ("zero" through "nine").
  * Punctuation Rule: Complete sentences must end with a period (.). Fragments, bulleted phrases,
    and incomplete statements must NOT have periods.
  * Emphasis: Do not use quotation marks for emphasis; use bold styling.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COGNITIVE LOAD MANAGEMENT & PROGRESSIVE DISCLOSURE (guidelines.md Section 3)
- Chunking: Maximum 3 to 4 content cards or bullet points per slide.
- Progressive Disclosure: Break multi-step architectures and pipelines into discrete steps.
  Reveal each step only when the SME discusses it, rather than presenting all items simultaneously.
- Anchor Function: Every on-screen card must function as a conceptual anchor, not a transcript.
